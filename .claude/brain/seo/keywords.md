# Keyword research (Google Autocomplete, 2026-10-01)

Raw autocomplete responses for every seed below are in `keywords_raw.json` (34 seeds,
committed verbatim). This file maps the demand signals to pages/content decisions.
Autocomplete gives demand signals (what people actually type), not search volumes — no
paid keyword tool (Semrush/Ahrefs) is connected in this session.

## Split Expense (`/split` + split guides/posts)
Primary: `split expense calculator`, `split bill calculator`
Long-tails with real demand: `split expenses calculator free`, `split expenses calculator
online free`, `split expenses calculator app`, `split bill calculator free online`,
`how to split bills with roommates`, `how to split utility bills with roommates`,
`how to split bills with friends`
Competitor demand (strong): `splitwise alternative` (+ free/india/open source/reddit
variants), `splitwise vs` (settle up, tricount, venmo, splid), `is splitwise safe`
(+ "to add bank account"/"to connect bank" — trust/privacy is a real concern people
search), `splitwise free` (+ "free limit"/"free vs pro" — Splitwise's free tier caps at
3 groups/month, a real, factual, publicly documented limitation)
Competitor demand (secondary): `tricount alternative` (+ open source/self-hosted),
`settle up app` (+ vs splitwise, alternative)

## Budget Tracker (`/budget` + budget guides/posts)
Primary: `budget tracker`, `budget tracker app`, `budget planner online`
Long-tails: `budget tracker free`, `budget tracker online free`, `how to budget money
for beginners`, `how to budget money on low income`
Competitor demand (strong): `ynab alternative` (+ reddit/free/uk/australia/open source),
`ynab vs` (monarch, rocket money, everydollar, actual budget, copilot, quicken — the
real competitive set for budgeting apps), `mint budget app alternative` (+ "budget apps
like mint", "is mint the best budgeting app" — Mint (Intuit) shut down in March 2024,
so "mint alternative" demand is from displaced users, a clean non-disparaging angle),
`every dollar budget app` (+ cost/premium — EveryDollar's paid tier is a real,
factual comparison point)

## Recurring Bills (`/bills` + bills guides/posts)
Primary: `recurring bill tracker`, `recurring expense tracker`
Long-tails: `recurring bill tracker excel`, `free recurring bill tracker`, `apps to keep
track of bills`, `how to track subscriptions` (+ "on iphone"/"for free" — subscription
creep is a recurring theme, already covered by the existing "audit your recurring bills"
post)
No strong single-competitor demand surfaced for this category (fragmented market: no
one "Splitwise of bill tracking" that people search against by name).

## Savings Goal (`/savings` + savings guides/posts)
Primary: `savings goal calculator`
Long-tails: `savings goal calculator with interest`, `savings goal calculator monthly`,
`savings calculator how long to reach goal`, `how to save money each month` (+ "from
salary"/"for students"/"calculator")
No strong single-competitor demand surfaced (savings calculators are mostly bank-site
utilities, not branded apps people compare against).

## Decisions this data supports
1. Add FAQ/H2 content phrased from these exact long-tails to existing posts/guides
   where it fits naturally (e.g. Splitwise's free-tier cap, Mint's 2024 shutdown).
2. "Alternative to X" pages have real, verifiable demand for: Splitwise (split
   category), Tricount (split category), YNAB (budget category), Mint (budget
   category, and factually defunct so zero disparagement risk). EveryDollar and
   Settle Up have secondary demand. Final list needs sign-off (naming competitors,
   even factually, needs explicit approval per the task brief).
3. Recurring Bills and Savings Goal have no comparable single-competitor demand —
   those categories should get more standalone guide/blog depth instead of
   alternative-to pages.
